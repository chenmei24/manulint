# Synthetic example — Diagnostic Performance of Dual-Energy CT for the Detection of Pulmonary Embolism in a Large Cohort of Patients With Suspected Acute Pulmonary Embolism in Emergency Department Settings

## Abstract
Background: Pulmonary embolism (PE) is a common cardiovascular emergency， early diagnosis is critical.  Dual-energy CT (DECT) has shown promise but large-scale validation remains limited.
Methods: We retrospectively enrolled patients with suspected PE who underwent DECT.  Image analysis was performed by two radiologists.
Results: A total of n=1200 patients were included.  The sensitivity was 94.2% and specificity was 96.8%.  P=0.03 for the comparison between DECT and conventional CT.  The difference was significant with p=0.000 for subgroup analysis.
Conclusion: DECT demonstrates high diagnostic performance for PE detection.

dual-energy CT, pulmonary embolism, diagnostic performance

## Introduction
Pulmonary embolism affects millions of people worldwide each year. Early and accurate diagnosis reduces mortality. Computed tomography pulmonary angiography (CTPA) is the reference standard. As shown in Figure 1, DECT provides material decomposition images. Fig. 2 shows iodine maps.

## Methods
This retrospective study included consecutive patients. Two radiologists independently reviewed the images. Statistical analysis: differences between groups were compared. A P value less than 0.05 was considered significant.

## Results
Among 1200 patients, 320 (26.7%) were diagnosed with PE. The area under the curve was 0.97. Interobserver agreement was good (kappa=0.81).
